# nyc-subway-connectivity-analysis
SQL and Tableau analysis of subway connectivity, ridership demand, and infrastructure gaps across NYC boroughs using synthetic data. 

## Background & Research 
My interest in the healthcare industry, combined with my experience living in New York City and regularly relying on its subway system, sparked an interest in understanding how public transportation connectivity may affect access to essential services, particularly healthcare facilities.

For this project, I selected a synthetic NYC subway database from several datasets provided in my graduate data management course and developed my own research question: How well does subway connectivity align with passenger demand across NYC boroughs? 

To provide real-world context for this analysis, I reviewed two studies that examine transportation accessibility and equity in New York City:
1. **[An Unhealthy Commute (2018)](https://nycfuture.org/research/an-unhealthy-commute)** - *Center for an Urban Future*. Examines transportation challenges affecting NYC healthcare workers, including long commute times, transit gaps, and limited subway access near some healthcare facilities.
2. **[Assessing Spatial and Racial Equity of Subway Accessibility (2024)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0264275124007030)** - *Academic study published in Cities*. Examines geographic disparities in NYC subway accessibility and evaluates how transit access varies among communities.

These studies provide historical context for understanding transportation inequalities and demonstrate why examining connectivity and passenger demand can be valuable for infrastructure planning.

**Scope and Limitations:** This project uses a synthetic academic dataset. Its findings illustrate an analytical approach and should not be interpreted as measurements of actual NYC subway conditions or healthcare accessibility.
